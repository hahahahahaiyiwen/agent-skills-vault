# Hierarchical Analysis

## Purpose

**Concept mapping**, as described by Novak and Canas, provides an established
framework for organizing knowledge through concepts and labeled relationships.
This skill focuses on its general-to-specific organization and cross-links,
not every meaning of hierarchical analysis.

The framework helps a reader understand how an explanation fits together or
where an existing document obscures relationships. It concerns conceptual
structure, not indentation or a required sequence of agent reasoning.

## Core model

- **Focus question:** the question or context that bounds the explanation.
- **Concept:** a named idea, category, or regularity, rather than an entire
  paragraph hidden inside a label.
- **Proposition:** concepts connected by a named relationship that forms a
  meaningful statement, such as "monitoring detects failures."
- **Hierarchy:** more inclusive concepts related to more specific concepts,
  with generality interpreted relative to the focus question.
- **Cross-link:** a meaningful relationship between different branches.
- **Example:** a particular instance that illustrates a concept, distinguishable
  from the concept itself.

Hierarchy is an organizing backbone, not a claim that knowledge is a pure tree.
Cross-links preserve relationships that a single parent-child arrangement
cannot express.

## What makes the structure informative

- **Vertical meaning:** each link says how the concepts relate. "Is a kind of,"
  "is part of," "supports," and "is measured by" are different relationships,
  not interchangeable meanings of a line or heading.
- **Comparable groupings:** concepts presented as peers have intelligible roles.
  Mixing goals, mechanisms, and measures without distinguishing them can hide
  rather than explain the subject.
- **Readable propositions:** relationship labels carry meaning beyond "related
  to." Their scope and certainty match the available support.
- **Honest cross-links:** shared concepts and dependencies remain visible
  without implying that repeated information is additional evidence.

The value lies in making relationships inspectable. A taller tree or a tidier
outline does not by itself improve the explanation.

## Example

A proposal lists "reduce operating cost," "shared storage," and "monthly
storage spend" as equivalent ideas. They are a goal, a proposed mechanism,
and a measure. Labeled relationships reveal how the mechanism is intended to
serve the goal and that the measure covers only part of operating cost.
Whether shared storage actually reduces total cost remains an evidence question.

This review examines the model behind a human- or agent-authored proposal,
not merely its headings.

## Boundaries

Concept mapping does not require conclusion-first writing, mutually exclusive
branches, or an exhaustive taxonomy. A diagram is optional when explaining or
reviewing these relationships; no fixed reasoning procedure is prescribed.

Preserve author intent and distinguish requirements, assumptions, evidence,
and inference. Missing relationships can remain unknown. Structure does not
establish truth, causality, or approval, nor justify invented content or extra
work solely to fill a branch.
